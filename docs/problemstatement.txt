## Problem Statement — Google Photos Semantic Memory Discovery

**Users can store thousands of photos in Google Photos, but finding a specific memory can become difficult when they remember the context of the moment rather than the exact photo, date, or filename.**

For example, a user may remember:

> *“I’m looking for those photos from Goa when we went to a café with friends in the evening.”*

They may remember **the place, activity, people, and approximate time**, but not the exact date or which photo was taken.

Today, photo discovery largely requires users to translate that vague memory into a search query and then scan through potentially relevant results. This can make the process feel more like **searching for a file than revisiting a memory**.

### Core problem

> **How might we help users find a specific memory in their photo library when they remember the context of the experience but not the exact photo or date?**

### User needs

Users should be able to:

* Describe a memory naturally using the clues they remember.
* Combine multiple contextual clues such as **place + activity + people + time**.
* Discover **where that memory exists in their photo history**.
* See the surrounding photos and chronological context.
* Move between relevant moments rather than manually scanning their entire library.

### Product opportunity

Instead of treating photo search purely as:

**“Which photos match my query?”**

the experience can shift toward:

**“Where in my photo history is the memory I'm trying to find?”**

That distinction is the foundation of your **Semantic Memory Timeline** concept.

### One-line version for your case study

> **Google Photos users often remember the context of a memory—where they were, what they were doing, who they were with, or roughly when it happened—rather than the exact photo or date, making contextual memories difficult to locate efficiently.**
